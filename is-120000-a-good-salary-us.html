Is $120,000 a Good Salary in the US?

A $120,000 salary is a strong income in the US and, for many households, sits comfortably above the level needed for basic financial stability. Even so, whether it feels genuinely strong depends on where you live, how much you pay in tax, and whether your housing, childcare, debt, and lifestyle costs are modest or heavy.

Quick answer: yes, $120,000 is a good salary in the US. For a single person it is often very comfortable, and for families it is usually solid to strong depending on the state and cost structure. On a simple federal-only estimate, $120,000 works out to about $96,299 after tax per year, or roughly $8,025 per month.

Why gross and net pay are different

  • Federal income tax: a larger salary means more income is taxed through higher brackets, even with the standard deduction applied.
  • FICA taxes: Social Security and Medicare are normally withheld from wages.
  • State tax variation: six figures plus in Texas or Florida can feel noticeably stronger than the same gross salary in California or New York.
  • Payroll deductions: 401(k), health insurance, HSA, and other benefits all affect net pay.
  • Household circumstances: filing status, dependents, and other deductions can change the effective take-home amount.

Is $120,000 a good salary in the US?

Yes. In most parts of the country, $120,000 is a clearly good salary and often a strong one. For a single adult, it usually supports a very comfortable standard of living. For couples and families, it often means solid financial stability and better room for saving, housing choice, and retirement contributions.

That said, it is not automatically “rich” everywhere. In high-cost metros, a $120,000 salary can still be pulled down by expensive rent or mortgages, childcare, commuting, and state tax. So while it is a good salary nationally, how good it feels depends heavily on the local cost of living and household setup.

For a single person

For a single adult, $120,000 is usually a very strong salary in the US. With an estimated $8,025 per month after federal taxes, there is often enough room for good housing, regular saving, investing, travel, and a more comfortable overall lifestyle.

In many areas, this is the salary level where financial choices open up rather than narrow down.

For a couple or family

For households with children, $120,000 is typically solid to strong, especially outside the most expensive areas. It can support a stable budget, family expenses, and longer-term financial planning much more comfortably than lower middle-income levels.

In the highest-cost areas, though, childcare, housing, and transport can still make the salary feel more “good” than “wealthy.”

What can change your take-home pay?

  • 401(k) contributions: these can materially reduce taxable income and improve retirement savings.
  • Health insurance and benefits: benefit deductions can be significant at this salary level.
  • Filing status: tax treatment changes depending on whether you are single, married filing jointly, or head of household.
  • State income tax: a major reason identical salaries produce different monthly take-home pay.
  • Bonuses and variable compensation: annual earnings may exceed base salary and affect withholding.
  • Pre-tax deductions: HSA, FSA, and similar benefits reduce taxable wages.
  • Debt obligations: mortgages, student loans, and car finance shape how comfortable the salary actually feels.
  • Household costs: childcare, commuting, rent, and insurance can have as much impact as tax.
