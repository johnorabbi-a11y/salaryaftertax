What affects take-home pay on an $83,000 salary in Texas?

No state income tax

This is the main Texas advantage. On the same gross salary, you keep more of your paycheck than you would in many other states, which improves the monthly and weekly feel immediately.

Housing market differences

Texas is not one single cost environment. Some areas are still relatively affordable, while others have seen major rent and home price growth. That can change how comfortable this salary feels.

Transport and commuting

Texas often means driving. Fuel, insurance, maintenance, and commute distance can become a meaningful part of your monthly costs, especially in large metro areas.

Healthcare and payroll deductions

Even without state income tax, your actual paycheck can be lower than expected if you have significant health insurance costs, 401(k) contributions, or other pre-tax and post-tax deductions.

Property tax and homeownership

Texas does not tax income at the state level, but homeowners often feel the trade-off through property taxes. That matters more if you are buying rather than renting.

Debt, family costs, and saving targets

Childcare, student loans, credit cards, and savings goals all shape how far this salary really goes. The clean tax structure helps, but household obligations still define the final lifestyle outcome.
