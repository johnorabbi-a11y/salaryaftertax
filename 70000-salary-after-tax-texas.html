Why Texas take-home pay is stronger

Compared with states such as California and New York, Texas often produces noticeably higher net pay because there is no state income tax to reduce your paycheck further.

  • Federal tax still takes a meaningful share of income.
  • FICA taxes still apply through Social Security and Medicare.
  • Texas state tax does not apply, which improves take-home pay.
  • Your real result can still move depending on benefits, retirement contributions, and tax filing status.

What can change your $70k take-home?

  • Filing status such as single, married filing jointly, or head of household
  • Pre-tax deductions like 401(k), medical insurance, FSA, or HSA
  • Bonus income, overtime, or uneven pay timing
  • Extra withholding choices on your W-4
  • Employer-specific benefits and payroll deductions

Is $70,000 a good salary in Texas?

$70,000 in Texas is often a solid salary, especially when compared with higher-tax states. In many parts of Texas, the lack of state income tax helps your paycheck stretch further for housing, fuel, family costs, and savings.

That said, comfort still depends on your local cost of living, household size, debt commitments, and whether you are contributing heavily to retirement or benefits through payroll.
