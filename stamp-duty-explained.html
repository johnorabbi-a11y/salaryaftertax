Stamp Duty Explained

Stamp duty is one of the major upfront costs that can change home-buying affordability in the UK. It does not affect monthly take-home pay like income tax, but it can materially change the deposit, moving budget and timing of a purchase.

What stamp duty is

Stamp duty is a property transaction tax paid when buying certain property in the UK. The amount depends on the property price, buyer status and current rules. It is separate from income tax, but it matters because it affects the cash needed to complete a purchase.

First-time buyer framing

First-time buyers may receive different treatment depending on current thresholds and eligibility. The practical point is that buyers should not compare salary to house price without also allowing for stamp duty, legal fees, moving costs and a post-move cash buffer.

Why salary matters

Salary and take-home pay influence mortgage affordability, but stamp duty influences upfront affordability. A household may be able to afford the monthly mortgage but still need more cash to cover transaction costs.

Moving costs and planning

Stamp duty should be planned alongside deposit, survey fees, conveyancing, removals, furnishing, repairs and emergency savings. Treating it as part of the wider housing budget avoids overcommitting at completion.

Questions this page helps answer

Why is salary alone not enough for affordability?

Salary does not show tax, deductions, debts, housing costs, insurance, transport, childcare or local cost differences.

Should I use gross salary or take-home pay?

Gross salary is useful for broad comparison, but take-home pay is usually better for monthly housing and budgeting decisions.

Does this apply to both UK and US users?

Yes, but the details differ. UK users may need stamp duty, PAYE and NI context, while US users often need state tax, property tax and insurance context.

Is this mortgage advice?

No. It is salary-linked affordability guidance. Mortgage products, tax rules and personal borrowing decisions should be checked with qualified professionals.
