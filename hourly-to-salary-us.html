US hourly to salary FAQ

How do you convert hourly pay into annual salary in the US?
Multiply hourly wage by hours worked per week and then by weeks worked per year. For example, $25 an hour at 40 hours a week over 52 weeks gives a gross annual pay estimate of $52,000 before tax.
Does this calculator include federal tax?
Yes. It estimates federal income tax using filing status and a standard deduction model.
Does it include Social Security and Medicare?
Yes. FICA is included, using Social Security and Medicare rates. Social Security is capped at the wage base while Medicare continues above that.
Can I choose a state?
Yes. California, Texas, Florida, New York, and Illinois are included. Texas and Florida have no state income tax in this estimate.
Can I include a 401(k)?
Yes. Enter a 401(k) percentage to reduce your estimated take-home and improve realism.
Why should there be a separate US version?
A dedicated US calculator page is cleaner for search intent, makes internal linking easier into US salary clusters, and reduces mixed UK and US relevance on a single URL.

Hourly pay is not just annual salary divided neatly

Hourly workers often live with more timing uncertainty than a simple annualised number suggests. Overtime, unpaid gaps, shift availability, seasonal work and benefit eligibility can all change what the wage really supports.

A higher hourly rate can be meaningful, but only if the hours are dependable. Someone earning less per hour with stable hours and benefits may sometimes have a stronger financial position than someone with a higher headline rate and volatile scheduling.

Hours risk

Annualised salary only works if hours are steady.

Overtime dependency

A budget that requires overtime is more fragile than one supported by base hours.

Benefits gap

Health insurance, PTO and retirement access can be as important as hourly rate.
