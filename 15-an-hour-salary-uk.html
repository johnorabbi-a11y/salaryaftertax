FAQ

Does £15 an hour always equal £29,250 a year?

No. The estimate assumes 37.5 paid hours per week for 52 weeks. Part-time hours, unpaid leave, overtime, and variable shifts will change annual pay.

Why compare hourly pay with monthly take-home pay?

Monthly take-home pay shows whether the hourly wage supports rent, bills, commuting, food, savings, and other recurring costs.

Should hourly workers use annual salary pages?

Yes, once the annual equivalent is known. Annual salary pages provide a clearer view of income tax, National Insurance, and net-pay comparisons.

How to use the £15 hourly estimate

Hourly pay should be interpreted with more care than a fixed salary because hours can vary. The annual estimate assumes steady full-time work, but many hourly workers have overtime, reduced schedules, unpaid breaks, shift variation, seasonal changes, or different weekly patterns.

For £15 an hour, the monthly take-home estimate is useful for checking recurring costs, but the weekly view may be just as important if pay is received weekly or if hours change from one rota to the next. That is why this page now links into both salary conversion tools and annual salary pages.

Users comparing hourly rates should also consider pension contributions, National Insurance, tax code accuracy, commuting costs, and whether paid holiday is included in the quoted rate. A higher hourly wage can still feel tight if hours are inconsistent or if travel costs are high.

The stronger role for this page is to bridge hourly-intent searches into the salary ecosystem. From here, users can compare annual equivalents, check after-tax salary pages, and move into monthly budgeting content without hitting a shallow utility page.
