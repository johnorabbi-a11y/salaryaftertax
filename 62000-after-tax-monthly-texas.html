Why take-home pay is relatively strong in Texas

Texas is often attractive from an after-tax income perspective because it has no state income tax. For salaries like $62,000, that means your main deductions are typically federal income tax, Social Security, and Medicare.

That does not mean overall living costs are always low. Depending on where you live in Texas, housing, property taxes, car costs, insurance, childcare, and commuting can still have a major impact on how far your paycheck goes each month.

  • No Texas state income tax: a key reason monthly take-home is competitive.
  • Federal tax still applies: your taxable income is reduced by the standard deduction first.
  • FICA is unavoidable for most employees: Social Security and Medicare still take a meaningful share from payroll.
  • Benefits matter: health insurance, retirement contributions, and HSA deductions can reduce net pay further.

Is $62,000 a good salary in Texas?

A $62,000 salary in Texas is generally a decent middle-income salary. For a single person in many parts of the state, it can support a comfortable standard of living, especially if housing costs are reasonable and debt levels are manageable.

In more expensive areas or for larger households, $62k can feel tighter once rent or mortgage payments, childcare, transport, groceries, and insurance are taken into account. Because Texas has no state income tax, it often performs better on a take-home basis than many people expect.
