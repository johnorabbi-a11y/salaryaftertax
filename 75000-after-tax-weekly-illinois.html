Why Does Illinois Reduce Weekly Take-Home?

The key difference is that Illinois applies state income tax on top of federal deductions and FICA. That means more gets taken out from each week’s earnings before you receive your net pay.

At the same $75,000 salary, workers in Texas and Florida usually keep more per week because those states do not tax wage income at the state level.

Illinois often sits between the two ends of the spectrum in salary comparisons: weaker than no-tax states, but sometimes a little less aggressive than higher-tax examples. That middle position is why it can be a useful benchmark state.

What Can Change Your Weekly Take-Home?

The estimate above is a strong baseline, but actual weekly take-home pay may vary depending on:

  • Payroll timing: weekly, biweekly, or semimonthly pay structures can change the size of individual payslips.
  • 401(k) contributions: pre-tax retirement deductions can reduce taxable income.
  • Health and benefit deductions: workplace benefits can reduce visible take-home pay each week.
  • Bonus or commission income: extra earnings can be withheld differently from normal salary.
  • Filing status and dependents: withholding choices can move the final weekly figure up or down.
