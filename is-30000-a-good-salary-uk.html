Is £30,000 a Good Salary in the UK?

A salary of £30,000 per year is around the lower-middle part of the full-time salary range in the UK. Whether it feels like a good salary depends heavily on where you live, your housing costs, and whether you are supporting a family on one income.

Monthly Take-Home Pay on £30,000

After Income Tax and National Insurance, a salary of £30,000 usually gives you around:

  • £2,000 to £2,100 per month take-home pay
  • Roughly £24,000 to £25,000 per year after tax

Is £30,000 Enough to Live Comfortably?

For many people, £30,000 is a decent salary, especially outside the most expensive parts of the UK. It can support a modest but stable lifestyle if your rent or mortgage is manageable.

  • Reasonable for a single person in many towns and cities
  • More stretched in London and the South East
  • Can cover essentials, bills, transport and some leisure spending
  • Savings may be limited if housing costs are high

How £30k Compares With Other Salaries

Compared with the UK average salary, £30,000 is roughly around average or slightly below average depending on the measure used. It is not a high salary, but it is also not low in many parts of the country.

  • Lower than £40,000 salary
  • Below £50,000 salary comfort levels
  • A useful baseline for comparing progression

What Kind of Lifestyle Does £30,000 Support?

On £30,000, many people can cover:

  • Rent or mortgage in lower-cost areas
  • Food, utilities and transport
  • Basic social life and subscriptions
  • Small monthly savings in favourable circumstances

If you are in a dual-income household, £30,000 can feel much more comfortable. As a sole earner with children or high rent, it may feel tighter.

Final Verdict

Yes, £30,000 can be a good salary in the UK for a single person in a lower-cost area, but it is more of a steady, workable income than a highly comfortable one. It becomes much more manageable when combined with lower housing costs or another household income.
