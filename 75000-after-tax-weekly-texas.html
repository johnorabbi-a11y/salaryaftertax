Why Does Texas Improve Weekly Take-Home?

The main factor is that Texas has no state income tax. Federal tax and FICA still apply, but there is no extra state-level wage tax reducing the weekly result.

On the same $75,000 salary, workers in Texas will usually keep more each week than workers in California, New York, or Illinois.

This makes Texas one of the more attractive states from a pure take-home-pay perspective. Even so, the wider financial picture still depends on housing, transport, insurance, childcare, and other regional living costs.

What Can Change Your Weekly Take-Home?

The estimate above is a clean baseline, but your actual weekly pay can move up or down depending on several factors:

  • Payroll schedule: some employers pay weekly, others biweekly or semimonthly, which changes how each paycheck looks.
  • 401(k) contributions: pre-tax retirement contributions can reduce federal taxable income.
  • Health and benefit deductions: insurance and other payroll deductions can reduce visible take-home pay.
  • Bonuses and extra earnings: supplemental wages can be withheld at different rates.
  • Filing status and dependents: your withholding elections can change the final weekly figure.
