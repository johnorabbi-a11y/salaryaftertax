Why Does Texas Come Out Better After Tax?

The biggest reason is simple: Texas does not charge state income tax. That means more of the salary stays in your pocket after payroll deductions are taken out. On the same $75,000 salary, a worker in Texas will usually take home more than a worker in California, New York, or Illinois.

Texas is often one of the stronger states for take-home pay comparisons because the salary only gets reduced by federal tax and FICA, not by an additional state income tax layer.

That does not automatically mean Texas is better in every situation, because cost of living, housing, commuting, insurance, and local property taxes can still shape how far pay goes. But from a pure income tax point of view, Texas is usually one of the more efficient states for net pay.

What Can Change Your Take-Home Pay?

The estimate above gives a strong baseline, but actual take-home pay can still vary. Common reasons include:

  • 401(k) contributions: pre-tax retirement contributions can reduce taxable wages for federal income tax purposes.
  • Health insurance and benefits: payroll deductions for medical, dental, and vision plans can lower your final paycheck.
  • Filing status and dependents: married filing jointly, head of household, or child-related credits can change withholding.
  • Bonuses and overtime: variable earnings are often taxed or withheld differently from base pay.
  • Employer payroll schedules: weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, or monthly payroll can change what each check looks like.
