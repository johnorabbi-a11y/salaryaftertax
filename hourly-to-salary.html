How the calculator works

1) Gross pay

We convert your hourly wage into annual pay using your weekly hours and weeks worked. Overtime and bonus are then added separately.

2) Estimated deductions

For the UK, the tool estimates income tax, National Insurance and student loan. For the US, it estimates federal tax, FICA and selected state tax.

3) Net take-home

The calculator then shows estimated yearly, monthly and weekly take-home pay, plus a breakdown you can compare across scenarios.

Hourly wage to salary FAQ

How do you convert hourly pay into annual salary?
Multiply your hourly wage by your hours per week and then by the number of weeks you work each year. For example, 40 hours a week at 20 per hour over 52 weeks gives a gross annual pay figure before tax and deductions.
Does this calculator show take-home pay or just gross salary?
It shows both. You get a gross annual figure and an estimated net pay figure after the selected deductions and tax logic are applied.
Can I include overtime and bonus?
Yes. Add average overtime hours per week and choose an overtime multiplier, then add any annual bonus. These are included in the gross estimate and flow through to the deduction estimates.
How accurate is the UK estimate?
It is designed as a strong estimate using UK tax bands, National Insurance and selected student loan logic. Real payroll can differ because of tax code changes, salary sacrifice, pension setup, benefits in kind and employer payroll treatment.
How accurate is the US estimate?
It is a solid estimate using federal tax, Social Security, Medicare, and selected state tax logic. Real take-home can vary due to filing details, local taxes, healthcare deductions, retirement plan choices and payroll timing.
Should pension or 401(k) be included?
Yes, if you want a more realistic take-home estimate. A pension or 401(k) contribution reduces the cash you take home, even though it can also reduce taxable income in some cases.

Questions about using this salary

Why does this page link to salary examples?

Salary examples turn abstract guidance into practical take-home-pay context. They help users move from a broad question to a specific annual, monthly or weekly income estimate.

Should I use annual, monthly or weekly pay?

Annual pay is best for job comparison, monthly pay is best for bills and affordability, and weekly pay is useful when income or spending is managed on a shorter cycle.

Are the linked salary pages a replacement for advice?

No. They are practical estimates and explainers. Personal circumstances such as pension contributions, student loans, benefits, bonuses and tax code changes can alter take-home pay.
