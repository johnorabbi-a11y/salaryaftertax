Payslip Explained

A payslip shows how gross pay becomes take-home pay. It is the practical record of salary, tax, payroll deductions, pension or retirement contributions, benefits and any timing differences that affect what reaches the bank account.

Why this page exists

Authority support

This guide helps explain why a salary-after-tax calculator can be useful while still differing from an actual payslip. Payroll is personal; calculator results are planning estimates built from standard assumptions.

The final take-home amount after deductions. This is the figure most useful for budgeting.

What a payslip usually shows

Most payslips separate gross pay, taxable pay, tax deductions, payroll taxes, pension or retirement deductions, benefits, employer information and the final net pay. The exact layout differs by employer and country, but the core idea is the same: show how the paycheck was calculated.

Why calculator estimates differ from payroll

A calculator may not know your exact tax code, filing status, dependents, pension contribution, 401(k), health premium, student loan plan, taxable benefits, local tax or bonus treatment. That is why AfterTaxTool presents salary pages as planning estimates rather than exact payroll statements.

Overtime, bonuses and timing can distort a payslip

Overtime and bonuses can be withheld differently from regular salary, especially in the US. In the UK, pension salary sacrifice, student loan deductions and tax code changes can also shift take-home pay. A single payslip may not represent the whole year if pay varies.

Questions this page helps answer

Why is my payslip different from a salary calculator?

Your payslip reflects personal payroll settings. A calculator uses standard assumptions, so differences can come from benefits, tax code, filing status, pension, student loans, bonuses or local taxes.

Should I trust a calculator or my payslip?

Use the payslip as the record of what was actually paid. Use the calculator for planning, comparison and understanding how gross salary may translate into take-home pay.

Can one payslip misrepresent my annual salary?

Yes. Bonuses, overtime, unpaid leave, timing differences or payroll corrections can make one payslip unusual. Annual estimates are better for broad comparison.

What should I check if take-home pay looks wrong?

Check tax code or filing details, pension or retirement deductions, benefit deductions, student loan or local tax lines, overtime treatment and whether the pay period includes unusual earnings.
