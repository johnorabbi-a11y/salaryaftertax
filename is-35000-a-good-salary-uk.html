Is £35,000 a Good Salary in the UK?

A salary of £35,000 per year is a respectable income in the UK and often sits around the national average for full-time workers. In many regions, it can support a fairly comfortable lifestyle for one person and a manageable household budget for couples.

Monthly Take-Home Pay on £35,000

After Income Tax and National Insurance, £35,000 usually leaves you with around:

  • £2,300 to £2,400 per month take-home pay
  • Roughly £27,500 to £29,000 per year after deductions

Is £35,000 a Comfortable Salary?

For a lot of people, £35,000 is a good salary. It usually covers everyday living costs, offers a bit more breathing room than £30k, and may allow for regular savings if rent or mortgage costs are sensible.

  • Comfortable for many single adults outside London
  • More stretched in expensive cities
  • Can support modest saving and occasional bigger purchases
  • Often enough for a balanced lifestyle with careful budgeting

How £35k Compares With the UK Average

£35,000 is close to or slightly above many national average salary figures, which makes it a useful benchmark salary. It is a clear step up from entry-level pay, though still well below higher-earning brackets.

  • Above £30,000 salary
  • Below £45,000 salary
  • A common target salary for career progression

What Lifestyle Can £35,000 Support?

On £35,000, people can often manage:

  • Private renting in many areas outside London
  • Normal household bills without major pressure
  • A car, modest travel and leisure spending
  • Small to medium monthly savings

For families, the comfort level depends much more on childcare and housing costs. For single earners in expensive areas, it may still feel tight.

Final Verdict

Yes, £35,000 is a good salary in the UK for many people, especially outside the highest-cost areas. It is a solid, realistic income that can provide stability, moderate comfort and some saving potential.
