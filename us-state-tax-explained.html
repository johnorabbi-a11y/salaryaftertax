US State Tax Explained

US state tax is one of the main reasons salary after tax varies across the country. A gross salary might look identical on two job offers, but the paycheck can change once state income tax, local taxes, benefits and cost-of-living realities are included.

Why this page exists

Authority support

Federal tax and state tax work together

Federal income tax and FICA usually apply before the state-specific comparison begins. State income tax then adds another layer where relevant. In a no-income-tax state, the payroll estimate may look stronger, but affordability still depends on rent, insurance, transport and local household costs.

No-income-tax states are not automatically simple

States such as Texas and Florida can improve paycheck visibility because there is no broad state income tax in the estimate. That advantage can be partly offset by other costs. Users should compare net pay and lived expenses rather than assuming no state tax means every salary feels easier.

Flat, progressive and local tax caveats

Some states use flatter income-tax structures, while others use progressive brackets. Certain cities or local jurisdictions can add their own complexity. AfterTaxTool state pages are designed for practical salary comparison, not full state tax-return preparation.

Questions this page helps answer

Why does the same salary have different take-home pay by state?

State income tax rules vary. Federal tax and FICA may be similar, but state tax, local tax and payroll assumptions can change the final net pay.

Do all states have income tax?

No. Some states do not apply broad state income tax, which is why state-specific salary pages are useful for comparison.

Are local taxes included?

Local taxes are not always included in simplified salary examples. Users with city-level or local payroll taxes should treat the result as a planning estimate.

Should I use this for relocation decisions?

It can help frame the tax side of a relocation decision, but housing, insurance, commuting and benefits should be compared too.
